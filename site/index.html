Invertebrate sentience and welfare in the scientific literature

Papers per year in which an invertebrate animal and sentience, emotion, consciousness, welfare or pain are discussed together, and how that compares with all research on the same animals. Counts come from OpenAlex and are updated each year by rerunning the code.

Preliminary. A paper counts when an animal word and a word about sentience, emotion, consciousness, welfare or pain appear within a few words of each other in its title or abstract. Some of those papers are still not about the animals' welfare (for example venom pharmacology, or consumer studies of insects as food). A manual check of a random sample will estimate the on-topic share; until then, treat each number as an approximation.

        How the numbers are made

        • Source. OpenAlex, queried on . Journal articles and reviews, from onward.
        • Two steps. First a broad search retrieves every paper that mentions an animal name (insect, crustacean, cephalopod, bee, shrimp, octopus, Drosophila and about 140 more) and a word about sentience, emotion, consciousness, welfare or pain in its title or abstract. Then a strict rule keeps a paper only when an animal word and one of those words occur within six words of each other, in any order ("insect welfare", "welfare of farmed insects"). Only those papers are counted. The lists of words are in config/config.yml.
        • Still an approximation. The strict rule removes most papers that merely mention both ideas, but not all of the unrelated ones. Counts also miss papers whose abstract is not available (about 5% of all records).
        • Share of the literature. Papers that meet the strict definition divided by all papers that mention the same animal group in the same year, with no condition on sentience or welfare. It corrects for the overall growth of publishing.

        Data: OpenAlex. Code and search terms: github.com/solimarygh/iwrs-welfare-stats. Rebuild the data with scripts/05_build_site_data.R.
